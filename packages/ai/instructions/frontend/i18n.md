# i18n conventions

## Components without an i18n library

For components meant for any stack.

- No i18n library in the package. The app gives an `I18nAdapter` in the configuration:
  `resolveText(namespace, key, params, defaultValue)`, `currentLocale()`, optional `onChange(listener)`.
  - Without an adapter: English texts and `en-US` formatting, always. The page's language (`<html lang>`) and the
    browser's are never read: the language comes only through the adapter.
  - How the app looks up a text is its own business (i18next, a plain object, …).
- Never name a specific i18n library in the package's code, docs or specs, except react-i18next as the example.
- A clash of a package's namespace with an app's own is solved in the app's adapter (e.g.
  ``ns: `components.${namespace}` ``).

## Components built on i18next

For components meant for apps with i18next and react-i18next.

- They use the app's instance (`I18nextProvider`).
- The package's texts (English and German) are i18next resources it exports (e.g. `dataTableTexts`), under its
  namespace; the app adds them to its instance: `resources: { en: { dataTable: dataTableTexts.en }, … }`.
- The package reads them typed by its own keys, not through the app's declared resource types (i18next's
  `CustomTypeOptions`): an app that types its own resources need not list the package's (e.g. a `useTexts()` hook of
  the package).

## Both

- The namespace of a package: its name in camelCase, no prefix: `'dataTable'`, `'fileUpload'`.
- Keys: camelCase, separated by dots (`home.attention.allGood`).
